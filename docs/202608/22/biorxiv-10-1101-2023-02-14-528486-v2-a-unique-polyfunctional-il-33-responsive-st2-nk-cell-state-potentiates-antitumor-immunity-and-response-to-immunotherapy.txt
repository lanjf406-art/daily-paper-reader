# ABSTRACT 39 

Despite increasing recognition of NK cell diversity, their functional heterogeneity and its relevance to 40 

cancer remain incompletely understood. Here, we show that IL-12 promotes the emergence of a distinct 41 

ST2/IL33R+ NK cell state poised for robust activation by IL-33. H uman ST2⁺ NK cells exhibit a unique 42 

transcriptional program, an intermediate differentiation state between CD56 bright and CD56 dim NK cells, 43 

and enhanced polyfunctionality combining proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxicity. ST2⁺ NK 44 

cells were identified across human and murine tumor datasets. In murine breast cancer models, IL-33 45 

and IL-12 cooperated to elicit potent antitumor responses dependent on IFN-γ and ST2⁺ NK cells. In 46 

cancer patients, IL-12 unleashed tumor-infiltrating ST2⁺ NK cells’ potential to produce IFN- in response 47 

to IL-33, an  IL33 hi -NK hi  score  was associated with prolonged survival , and ST2⁺ NK cells correlated with 48 

improved immunotherapy response. Collectively, these findings identify ST2⁺ NK cells as a49 

therapeutically actionable NK cell state promoting antitumor immunity. 50

# SIGNIFICANCE 59 

NK cell functional diversity  in cancer  remains  incompletely understood . Integrated transcriptional and 60 

functional analyses identify a unique IL -33 -responsive ST2+ NK cell  state representing  an intermediate 61 

differentiation  stage  with strong polyfunctional anti -tumor activity  and  favorable prognostic value  in 62 

solid tumors . IL -33 therefore represents a  novel therapeutic axis  for  harnessing NK cells  against cancer .63

# INTRODUCTION 65 

Natural Killer (NK) cells are  major  innate  effector cells  involved in host defense against viral 66 

infections and tumors  (1) , by directly killing  target  cells  but also by  supporting effective innate and 67 

adaptive immune responses  (2) . Accordingly, high intratumoral infiltration by NK cells is associated with 68 

improved prognosis of cancer patients  (3,4) . However, chronic exposure to activating signals, presence 69 

of inhibitory ligands and immunosuppressive molecules in tumor microenvironment favor NK cells 70 

dysfunction, leading to tumor escape  (1) . In this context, NK cells are emerging as promising targets for 71 

cancer therapy with a growing number of therapeutic agents  aiming to unleash their full antitumor 72 

potential. 73 

NK cells have  evolved into a spectrum of effector subsets  diverging  in terms of  phenotype, 74 

function, location, and responsiveness to activating stimuli to ensure complementary roles in immune 75 

response s (5) . Indeed,  in humans , CD56 bright  NK cells  are  predominant in lymphoid tissues,  secrete high 76 

levels of cytokines and chemokines including Interferon -gamma (IFN) -γ, Tumor Necrosis Factor -alpha 77 

(TNF -α), CCL3/4 (Macrophage Inflammatory Protein 1 α/β) , and  CCL5 (RANTES) and undergo robust 78 

proliferation after cytokine stimulation . Conversely,  CD56 dim  NK cells  are  the largest circulating 79 

population,  show a greater cytotoxic ability but a  lower  cytokine production and proliferation , and 80 

express the activating Fc receptor CD16 endowing them with antibody -dependent cellular cytotoxicity 81 

(ADCC)  function , a central  mechanism in  certain  antibody -based antitumor therapies .82

tumoral  context s (13 –15) . NK cell subsets  have  distinct transcriptional programs  that  are  governed  by 89 

profound  epigenetic modifications and chromatin re -arrangements  (16,17) . Yet, functional 90 

heterogeneity in tumoral context  and in response to cytokine stimuli from the tumor microenvironment 91 

remains to be fully uncovered. Recent evidence show that a subset of cytotoxic NK cells limits the 92 

efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade, by interfering with an efficient cytotoxic T cell response 93 

(18,19) . Therefore, NK cell  transcriptional and functional  diversity  extends beyond current paradigms, 94 

with distinct states differentially shaping antitumor immunity and clinical outcomes, providing new 95 

opportunities for therapeutic intervention .96 

The activation of NK cells depends on the  signaling  balance between activating and inhibitory 97 

receptors . However , numerous cytokines  such as  Interleukin(IL) -2, IL -12, IL -15, IL -18, and type I IFN  (20) 98 

also regulate NK cell  maturation, activation, and survival  by acting alone or  in cooperation . The IL -1-99 

familly  cytokine  IL -33  is  constitutively  expressed  in the nucleus of non -immune cells at  epithelial  barrier s100 

(21) . In response to tissue injury, cellular stress, and inflammation, IL -33  is released and  functions  as an 101 

alarmin by activating  immune cell s expressing  its  heterodimeric  receptor , composed of  ST2  (encoded by 102 

IL1RL1  gene) and ILRAcP accessory chain  (21) . The  IL -33/ST2 axis was  originally described  to support  type 103 

2 immunity  (22) , as well as a subset of regulatory T cells (Tregs) constitutively expressing ST2 and 104 

involved in tissue repair  (23)  and immunomodulation  (24) . In contrast , IL -33  has later  emerg ed  as a 105 

regulator of  type 1 immune  responses during viral infection and chronic immune pathologies in mice, 106 

enhancing IFN - production in CD4 + cells  and  CD8 + T cells  that acquire ST2 expression under 107 

inflammatory conditions  (25 –27) . In cancer context,  IL -33 has revealed opposing effects depending on 108

# DISCUSSION 302 

Previous studies have established  a central  role  for  the  IL -33 /ST2 pathway  in  type  2 immunity ,303 

yet  its  contribution  to  type  1 immune responses  and  antitumor immunity  has remained  poorly defined .304 

Here, we identify  a previously unrecognized  NK cell  state , termed ST2⁺ NK cells, that integrate s IL -12 and 305 

IL -33 signals to drive potent antitumor immunity. Positioned between  canonical  CD56 bright  and CD56 dim 

306 

NK cells, these  metabolically active,  highly proliferative , and polyfunctional effectors are present in 307 

human tumors  and contribute to tumor control  in mice  through an NK cell -, IFN -γ-, and ST2 -dependent, 308

Together , these findings establish  ST2⁺ NK cells  as key mediators of IL -33 -driven antitumor responses 311 

and  identify the IL -33/ST2/NK cell axis as  promising  target  for cancer immunotherapy. 312 

Mechanistically, we demonstrate that  IL -12  is a key upstream regulator of  ST2 expression in  both 313 

human and mouse NK cells . IL -12 signaling directly induces ST2  through STAT4 binding to the  Il1rl1 314 

promoter , thereby licensing NK cells to respond to IL -33 . Consistent with this mechanism , a  recent study 315 

showed  that  the  T-bet and STAT4  transcription factors  orchestrate  the activation of  Il1rl1  promote r in 316 

NK cells  and other  type -1 lymphocytes  during  viral infection  (43) . Our findings therefore position IL -12 317 

as a molecular switch that redirects IL -33 activity toward type 1 immunity.  Similar to  well -established IL -318 

12/IL -18 axis, which promotes IFN -γ production through STAT4/c -Jun complexes and p38 -MAPK 319 

activation  in human NK cells  (44,45) , IL -33  similarly  activates c -jun  (46)  and p38 -MAPK  signaling  (our 320 

data  and  (31) ),  providing a mechanistic basis for its synergy with IL -12 . However,  IL -33  extends beyond 321 

IFN -γ induction by promoting broad array inflammatory cytokines and chemokines production by  ST2+ 322 

CD56 dim  NK cells, while markedly enhancing their IL -2-driven proliferation, despite the intrinsically 323 

limited proliferative capacity of  conventional  CD56 dim  NK ce lls . These observations reveal a distinctive 324 

activation program that couple effector function and proliferation, which is of particular interest to fine -325 

tune the harnessing of NK cell effector functions. 326 

Single -cell transcriptomic  and functional  analys es position  ST2 + NK cells as an  IL -12 -induced  state 327 

within the NK cell differentiation continuum, bridging CD56 bright  and CD56 dim  NK cells. This model aligns 328 

with previous descriptions of intermediate NK populations defined by distinct phenotypic  (GZMK, XCL1, 329 

CD94/NKG2A, CD62L, CX3CR1, KIRs,  or  CD57  (6,8,9,47) ),  transcriptional , and  epigenetic  pro gram s330 

(12,14,16,48 –50) . Functionally, besides broad cytokine -producing capacity, ST2 + NK cells combine the 331 

proliferative and metabolic fitness of CD56 bright  NK cells with the effector functions of CD56 dim  NK cells, 332 

endowing them with an unusual degree of polyfunctionality. These features identify ST2 + NK cells as a 333 

distinct functional state with considerable therapeutic potential for NK -cell -based immunotherapy. 334
